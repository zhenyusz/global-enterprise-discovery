Part 1 · Orientation

Why discovery comes first

This lesson is for new and developing Sales Engineers who lead technical discovery with large, global enterprises — where stakeholder complexity, regional differences, and hybrid environments make the first conversation hard.

Learning objective

Scope a broad customer request, uncover the relevant workflow and stakeholders, and agree on an appropriate next technical action.

The customer’s opening

“We need better visibility into security risks across the company. We have too many tools and too many findings, but no consistent way to determine what needs immediate action.”

— Priya Raman, Global CISO (fictional)

Your task

Across the next five parts you will learn a discovery method, plan your approach, run the customer meeting, and receive a performance scorecard with targeted practice.

Coach’s note

Under pressure, the instinct is to reach for a product answer early. This lesson builds the opposite habit: understand first, so you can tell what a good solution must accomplish.

Part 2 · The Sales Engineer’s role

Know what you own

Discovery works best when you know which parts of the deal are yours to lead.

The SE leads

  • Technical discovery and technical fit
  • Architecture discussion
  • Tailored demonstrations
  • Technical validation
  • Proof-of-value success criteria

The SE does not primarily own

  • Pricing
  • Contract negotiation
  • Legal approval
  • Post-sale implementation

Lesson complete

  • Discovery is a sequence: understand the spark, context, workflow, pain, and evidence before proposing anything.
  • The SE’s credibility comes from restraint — earning the demo by understanding the problem first.
  • A strong meeting ends on an agreed next technical action and a shared definition of success.
“Strong Sales Engineers do not begin by presenting a solution. They begin by understanding the customer well enough to determine what a successful solution must accomplish.”
